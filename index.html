About

I'm a Computer Systems & Networks student at University Malaya, working out of a home lab I built rather than a tutorial I followed. The lab is the work: a two-node Proxmox cluster, a NAS, sixteen containers, and a monitoring setup that tells me when something breaks before I notice it myself.

Most of what I know about infrastructure came from things going wrong. Two separate faults could take the entire cluster offline and present almost identically from the outside — every VM losing network, the host unresponsive. The first fix I found online held for about a week, then didn't. The real cause was a second, unrelated layer I hadn't found yet. Getting to the bottom of that is the part I'm actually proud of.

I keep everything in a structured, cross-linked documentation vault that I treat as source of truth. It's not an afterthought — it's how I keep from repeating a fix I already applied twice.

Selected work

Two-node Proxmox cluster with a hard-won uptime

Infrastructure · Troubleshooting · Linux

A Proxmox cluster that kept dropping every VM's network at once. Two independent faults, near-identical symptoms:

  1. Intel I219-LM link drops. Firmware bug causing the NIC to lose link intermittently. EEE, ASPM and Wake-on-LAN all triggered it. Disabling those three held for days — then recurred, because a separate hardware-unit hang was being caused by packet offloads. Final fix disables EEE/ASPM/WoL and TSO/GSO/GRO, applied on boot.
  2. i915 crash on GPU passthrough handoff. The host hung when starting or stopping passthrough. Root cause was module load order; fixed by declaring i915 soft-dependent on vfio-pci.

The first fix didn't hold. Diagnosing the second layer underneath is the part that took the real work.
